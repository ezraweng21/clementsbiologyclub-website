# BioBox teacher form and outreach drafts

## Google Form to create

**Title:** BioBox Classroom Request & Teacher Questions

**Description:** Clements Biology Club's BioBox program provides hands-on, TEKS-aligned science activities and teacher grant-research support. Tell us what would help your class. We will confirm availability, timing, and school requirements before scheduling anything.

### Section 1 - About you

1. **Name** - short answer - required
2. **School** - short answer - required
3. **Best school email** - short answer - required - response validation: email
4. **Grade level(s) and course(s)** - checkboxes: 6th, 7th, 8th, Biology, Chemistry, Aquatic Science, Environmental Systems, Other - required
5. **Approximate students served** - short answer - required
6. **Preferred format** - multiple choice: 10-15 minute demonstration; 30-45 minute activity; full 45-55 minute investigation; multi-day follow-up; not sure
7. **Preferred timing / dates** - paragraph

### Section 2 - Request an existing BioBox activity

8. **Which collection are you browsing?** - multiple choice: Middle School; High School; Not sure
9. **Which BioBox activity or activities interest you?** - checkboxes - not required. Include the current catalog names, grouped by collection.
10. **What unit or TEKS are you teaching now?** - paragraph - required
11. **What equipment is already available?** - checkboxes: projector; balance; microscopes; sink; refrigerator; hot plate; none/limited; other
12. **Any safety, allergy, accessibility, schedule, or class-management needs?** - paragraph

### Section 3 - Request something not yet in BioBox

13. **Is there a science activity, supply, or piece of equipment you wish your students had?** - paragraph
14. **Approximate cost, if known** - short answer
15. **What learning goal or TEKS would it support?** - paragraph
16. **Would Teacher Grant Help be useful for this request?** - multiple choice: Yes, please contact me; Maybe, send more information; No thank you

### Section 4 - Ask a question

17. **What question do you have for the BioBox team?** - paragraph
18. **May we contact you about this request?** - multiple choice: Yes; No, this is only a general question - required
19. **Optional impact documentation preference** - checkboxes: teacher quote; non-identifying photo of materials/student work; no photos; ask me first. Include help text: “Photos are never required and we never request student names. With school permission, documentation helps us report impact for future grants.”

**Confirmation message:** Thank you. A Clements Biology Club officer will review your request and follow up using your school email. A submission is not a scheduled delivery; we will first confirm fit, materials, timing, and school requirements.

## General grant-answer template

**Organization:** Clements Biology Club is a student-led, teacher-supported program at Clements High School in Sugar Land, Texas. We connect students to biology beyond the textbook through hands-on labs, science communication, competition preparation, and community outreach.

**Use of funds:** Funding will support BioBox materials for free classroom activities, small prizes for student science writing, and student biology competitions such as Bio-Thon Open. We request support through the school/faculty sponsor or another eligible fiscal partner and will report materials purchased, students reached, teacher feedback, and permission-compliant documentation.

## Sponsor-email reality check

Yes: a sponsorship email usually asks for money, a gift card, supplies, or an event partnership. The strongest version asks for **one specific thing** and says what it will accomplish. For restaurants and chains, an official fundraiser/community form or local manager is often better than a generic corporate email. Do not promise tax deductibility unless the school or fiscal sponsor confirms it.

## Why middle-school contact research was sparse

Most FBISD middle-school sites currently use staff directories rather than detailed department pages. Many list names without direct subject/department filters, do not publish a science chair, and do not list student-club advisers publicly. Some older links from planning materials also point to pages that have moved after site redesigns. That is why the contact file has only emails that were visibly confirmed on official school pages, rather than guessed addresses. The safer next step is a principal/front-office or science-department inquiry that asks for the appropriate contact, not an attempt to infer emails.
