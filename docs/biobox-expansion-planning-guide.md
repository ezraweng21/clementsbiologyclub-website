# BioBox recommended expansion planning guide

Updated: 2026-08-16. This is an internal planning document, not website copy. It expands the 15 middle-school and 15 high-school additions from `biobox-teks-audit-and-expansion.md`.

## How to read the ratings

**Teacher-facing labels:** ease of incorporation and student engagement are each out of five stars. They are intentionally quick, not a claim that every class will use the same format.

**Internal selection scorecard:** `P/T/V/A/S/L/E/B/R/C` means **price, TEKS directness, visual payoff, active student role, safety, lesson-length flexibility, equipment independence, teacher-burden reduction, reusability/scalability, and classroom-management simplicity**. Each is 1-5. Price and TEKS are weighted three times; visual payoff, student role, safety, equipment independence, and teacher-burden reduction are weighted twice; the other four are weighted once. The resulting total is out of 100. A ready-to-offer item should normally score at least 80/100 and have a price score of at least 4/5.

**Price rule:** prices are planning ranges for a 30-student class, excluding shipping and sales tax. Buy only after comparing the final cart total. A low sticker price is not a good deal if it creates 30 separate kits, needs special equipment, or leaves unusable leftovers.

**TEKS rule:** “Verified” means the activity and code appear in the supplied BioBox catalog PDF. “Confirm before listing” means it is a promising concept but no code should be published until the course teacher confirms the current TEKS wording and unit fit.

## Middle school - 15 additions

### 1. Cheek Cell Microscopy

**Status/TEKS:** Verified - 6.13(A), 7.13(B), 8.13(A). **Planning price:** $6-$15/class when a school already has microscopes; about $0.20-$0.50/student for swabs, slides, and stain. **Ease:** ★★★★☆. **Engagement:** ★★★★☆. **Internal:** P4/T5/V4/A5/S4/L4/E2/B4/R3/C4 = **82/100**.

**Lesson use:** Students gently swab the inside of a cheek, prepare a slide, identify cell boundaries/nucleus, and submit a labeled biological drawing. It is a direct way to make “cells are the basic unit of life” observable rather than memorized.

**Best-deal plan:** First ask whether the teacher has microscopes, reusable slides, and methylene blue. If so, order a 100-pack of individually wrapped sterile cotton swabs plus 50-72 plastic slides/coverslips; use plastic slides for lowest breakage and paper-towel cleanup. Search “classroom microscope slides 72 pack” and “sterile cotton swab 100 count,” compare per-slide price, and buy enough for one slide per student plus 15% extras.

**Mini FAQ:** (1) *Do students have to share samples?* No; each student handles only their own slide/swab. (2) *Do we need microscopes?* Yes; offer it only to classes with enough stations, or use it as a teacher-demo rotation. (3) *What do students turn in?* A labeled cell sketch and one observation/evidence sentence.

### 2. Iodine Macromolecule Test

**Status/TEKS:** Verified - 6.6(E), 7.6(C), 8.6(E). **Planning price:** $5-$12/class, about $0.17-$0.40/student. **Ease:** ★★★★★. **Engagement:** ★★★★☆. **Internal:** P5/T5/V4/A4/S4/L5/E5/B5/R4/C5 = **95/100**.

**Lesson use:** Students predict which mystery foods contain starch, add iodine, record the blue-black versus amber result, and defend a claim using the color-change evidence. It supports observations of physical/chemical properties without requiring a long lab setup.

**Best-deal plan:** Buy one small bottle of iodine solution only if the school cannot supply it; food samples should come from a single low-cost grocery run (potato, bread, cracker, apple, water control). Use a 100-pack of clear plastic cups or a spot plate instead of individual test tubes. Search “Lugol iodine solution classroom” and compare concentration, bottle size, and hazard labeling; one 30-60 mL bottle normally supports several classes when drops are dispensed by the facilitator.

**Mini FAQ:** (1) *Is it messy?* Only a little; use trays and have BioBox dispense drops. (2) *Can it be a demo?* Yes, four samples can be done in 10 minutes. (3) *What is the student artifact?* A prediction-result-evidence table.

### 3. Owl Pellet Dissection

**Status/TEKS:** Verified - 6.12(A), 7.12(A), 8.12(A). **Planning price:** target $45-$85/class for 15-16 pellets plus tools; approximately $1.50-$3/student when pairs share one pellet. **Ease:** ★★★☆☆. **Engagement:** ★★★★★. **Internal:** P3/T5/V5/A5/S4/L4/E4/B4/R2/C3 = **76/100**. **Decision:** reserve for teacher-requested ecology units or grant-funded orders because price is above the usual BioBox target.

**Lesson use:** Pairs sort bones, infer prey, construct a food web, and explain how the pellet is evidence of an owl’s role in an ecosystem. The tangible evidence makes population and ecosystem relationships unusually memorable.

**Best-deal plan:** Compare sanitized/preserved bulk pellets by **cost per pellet**, not by kit price. Order 16 for a 30-student class (pairs) plus one spare, and reuse forceps/picks. Avoid individually boxed pellets unless a teacher needs the packaging. Check the vendor’s class-pack count, whether bone charts are included, and shipping; current Carolina class kits can be substantially more expensive than bulk pellets, so price both routes before ordering.

**Mini FAQ:** (1) *Is it safe?* Use commercially sanitized pellets, gloves, no blowing on specimens, and handwashing. (2) *Can students work in pairs?* Yes - that is the cost-smart format. (3) *How long does it take?* One 45-55 minute period or two shorter sessions.

### 4. Soil Testing

**Status/TEKS:** Verified - 6.12(A), 8.12(C). **Planning price:** $12-$20/class for a 20-test kit, about $0.40-$0.70/student in groups. **Ease:** ★★★★☆. **Engagement:** ★★★★☆. **Internal:** P4/T5/V3/A4/S5/L4/E5/B4/R3/C4 = **85/100**.

**Lesson use:** Students test two local soils or soil versus potting mix for pH/nutrients, graph the results, and make a claim about what conditions might support plant growth. Local samples make the data feel consequential.

**Best-deal plan:** Buy a 20-test garden soil kit only after confirming which measures it includes; pH alone is cheaper but less useful than pH plus N-P-K for an environmental investigation. One kit supports six to ten groups if each group tests one variable/sample. Search “soil test kit 20 tests pH NPK,” compare number of complete tests rather than number of vials, and collect soil locally instead of buying prepared samples.

**Mini FAQ:** (1) *Do we need a garden?* No; use campus, home, or potted-plant soil. (2) *Will results be exact?* Treat them as comparative classroom data, not a certified lab analysis. (3) *What is the output?* A data table, graph, and evidence-based recommendation.

### 5. Mealworm Metamorphosis

**Status/TEKS:** Verified - 6.12(B), 7.13(D), 8.13(C). **Planning price:** $8-$18/class for 50 mealworms, habitat, and food; $0.25-$0.60/student. **Ease:** ★★★☆☆. **Engagement:** ★★★★★. **Internal:** P4/T5/V4/A5/S4/L2/E5/B3/R3/C3 = **78/100**. **Decision:** good for a teacher willing to host a multi-week organism observation; not a drop-in kit.

**Lesson use:** Students observe and sketch larva, pupa, and adult stages over several weeks, then connect structure/behavior changes to life-cycle and survival claims. BioBox should provide a schedule and data sheet so the teacher does not have to invent the follow-up.

**Best-deal plan:** Order 50-100 mealworms from a local pet/feed supplier or classroom vendor, not individually packaged “science kits.” Use one ventilated reusable bin per class, oats/bran as bedding and food, and carrot slices for moisture. Search “live mealworms 100 count local pickup” first; local pickup cuts shipping risk. Order 50 for a shared class habitat, not one per student.

**Mini FAQ:** (1) *Who cares for them?* One class habitat needs a two-minute check every few days. (2) *Are there allergies?* Ask about insect/shellfish allergies and offer a no-contact data role. (3) *Can it fit one day?* Use a pre-established colony for a one-period observation, but metamorphosis needs follow-up.

### 6. Diffusion: Hot vs. Cold Water

**Status/TEKS:** Verified - 7.8(A), 7.8(C). **Planning price:** $2-$6/class, under $0.20/student. **Ease:** ★★★★★. **Engagement:** ★★★★☆. **Internal:** P5/T5/V5/A4/S5/L5/E5/B5/R5/C5 = **98/100**.

**Lesson use:** Students predict which cup will spread food coloring fastest, observe equal drops in hot and cold water, time a visible diffusion threshold, graph results, and explain the particle-motion connection. It is a strong controlled-experiment model.

**Best-deal plan:** Buy one multi-color food-coloring set and a sleeve of clear cups; use classroom hot water and room-temperature water. Search “food coloring 4 pack” and select the cheapest standard liquid set, not gel coloring. Buy 40 cups so groups can repeat a trial; almost all supplies remain for future lessons.

**Mini FAQ:** (1) *Is heat required?* Warm tap water is enough; avoid boiling water. (2) *Can students collect data?* Yes - timing and a shared class graph are the point. (3) *How long?* 25-35 minutes including explanation.

### 7. Crystal Growing

**Status/TEKS:** Verified - 7.6(E). **Planning price:** $4-$10/class, about $0.15-$0.35/student. **Ease:** ★★★★☆. **Engagement:** ★★★★☆. **Internal:** P5/T5/V4/A4/S4/L2/E5/B4/R4/C4 = **84/100**.

**Lesson use:** Students make a saturated solution, predict what will happen as water evaporates/cools, and observe crystal formation across days. The follow-up can compare temperature, container shape, or concentration.

**Best-deal plan:** Purchase a one-pound container of alum or borax only after checking school safety rules; alum is generally the simpler classroom choice. Use reusable clear cups/jars and string/pipe cleaners already on hand. Search “alum powder 1 lb” and compare food-grade or laboratory-grade only when purity is actually needed; one pound is far more than one class needs, so split across future sessions.

**Mini FAQ:** (1) *Do crystals form during class?* Setup fits one period; the best results appear later. (2) *Is it a take-home item?* It can be, after teacher approval. (3) *What do students record?* Initial conditions, daily observations, and a variable-based explanation.

### 8. Heart Rate Lab

**Status/TEKS:** Verified - 7.13(A). **Planning price:** $0-$8/class; almost free with timers and optional paper data sheets. **Ease:** ★★★★★. **Engagement:** ★★★★★. **Internal:** P5/T5/V3/A5/S5/L5/E5/B5/R5/C4 = **94/100**.

**Lesson use:** Students measure resting heart rate, make a prediction, complete a safe standardized movement interval, measure recovery, graph data, and explain how body systems respond to activity. Students can use anonymous group data if personal sharing is uncomfortable.

**Best-deal plan:** Do not buy heart-rate monitors for the first version. Use classroom timers/phones where permitted and a printed data sheet; one optional $8 digital timer improves station management but is not required. Buy only reusable clipboards/pencils if the club lacks them.

**Mini FAQ:** (1) *Is exercise required?* No; use a gentle, opt-in movement or analyze sample data. (2) *Is health data private?* Yes; do not collect names with heart-rate values. (3) *How long?* 35-45 minutes with graphing.

### 9. DIY Stethoscope

**Status/TEKS:** Verified - 7.13(A). **Planning price:** $10-$20/class, $0.35-$0.70/student. **Ease:** ★★★★☆. **Engagement:** ★★★★☆. **Internal:** P4/T5/V3/A5/S5/L4/E5/B4/R3/C4 = **85/100**.

**Lesson use:** Teams build a simple sound-transmission model, test which design makes a heartbeat easiest to hear, revise the design, and connect the result to a body-system model. It turns a health unit into an engineering iteration.

**Best-deal plan:** Use bulk flexible tubing, funnels, and disposable cups rather than prepackaged kits. Search “clear vinyl tubing 25 ft” and “small plastic funnels 12 pack,” then cut identical lengths for 15 pair kits. Buy 10% extra tubing for failed cuts and provide alcohol wipes if ear-contact components are shared.

**Mini FAQ:** (1) *Are shared earpieces hygienic?* Use individual ends or sanitize between users. (2) *Does it replace a real stethoscope?* No; it is a sound model. (3) *What is graded?* A labeled design, test result, and redesign rationale.

### 10. Mold Growth Investigation

**Status/TEKS:** Verified - 7.14(B), 8.12(B). **Planning price:** $5-$15/class, $0.17-$0.50/student. **Ease:** ★★☆☆☆. **Engagement:** ★★★★☆. **Internal:** P4/T5/V3/A4/S2/L2/E4/B2/R3/C2 = **66/100**. **Decision:** do not make this a standard offer; use only a sealed, teacher-approved observation protocol.

**Lesson use:** Students compare sealed samples under controlled conditions and analyze images/data without opening containers. It can illustrate microbial growth and variables, but safety and disposal make it more burdensome than agar plates.

**Best-deal plan:** If approved, buy resealable clear bags, gloves, labels, and bread from one grocery run; make one sample per group, not per student. Never optimize cost by using unsealed plates or opening samples. If the teacher lacks a safe disposal plan, choose agar-plate observation or a photo-data alternative instead.

**Mini FAQ:** (1) *Will students touch mold?* No; all samples stay sealed. (2) *Can we open the bags to identify it?* No. (3) *Who disposes of it?* The teacher/school follows its established disposal policy.

### 11. Brine Shrimp Life Cycle

**Status/TEKS:** Verified - 6.12(C), 7.13(C), 8.12(A). **Planning price:** $10-$25/class, $0.35-$0.85/student. **Ease:** ★★★☆☆. **Engagement:** ★★★★★. **Internal:** P4/T5/V4/A5/S4/L2/E3/B3/R3/C3 = **74/100**. **Decision:** excellent only when a teacher wants daily observations and can host a small aquarium/light setup.

**Lesson use:** Students hatch brine shrimp, count/observe developmental changes, graph survival, and connect environmental conditions to organism survival. It works best as a class investigation with shared cultures rather than individual containers.

**Best-deal plan:** Buy one small packet/tin of brine shrimp eggs, non-iodized salt or marine salt mix, and use two reused clear containers. Search “brine shrimp eggs 1 oz hatch rate” and compare price per gram plus stated hatch rate; avoid novelty kits with tiny packets. One ounce is generally more than enough for repeated class trials, so partner with another class or store dry eggs correctly.

**Mini FAQ:** (1) *Do we need an aquarium?* No; clear containers, salt water, light, and an air source/regular gentle mixing are enough for a small class setup. (2) *How long?* Hatching begins in 1-3 days; plan several observation days. (3) *Can students take them home?* Only if the teacher permits; a shared classroom habitat is simpler.

### 12. Penny Surface-Tension Trial

**Status/TEKS:** Verified - 8.6(C). **Planning price:** $2-$7/class, under $0.25/student. **Ease:** ★★★★★. **Engagement:** ★★★★☆. **Internal:** P5/T5/V4/A5/S5/L5/E5/B5/R5/C5 = **97/100**.

**Lesson use:** Students predict how many drops a penny can hold, run repeated drop-count trials, compare plain versus soapy water, calculate averages, and explain cohesive forces using evidence. It is a quantitative upgrade to a quick water-properties demo.

**Best-deal plan:** Collect clean pennies, use one bottle of dish soap, and buy 10-15 inexpensive plastic pipettes. Search “plastic transfer pipettes 100 pack”; one bulk pack can support many labs. Do not buy special droppers per student; one pipette per group is enough.

**Mini FAQ:** (1) *Does every group need a new penny?* No; rinse/dry between conditions. (2) *Will the data vary?* Yes, which is useful for discussing repeated trials and averages. (3) *How long?* 25-40 minutes.

### 13. Vinegar and Baking Soda Reaction

**Status/TEKS:** Verified - 6.6(E), 7.6(C). **Planning price:** $4-$10/class, about $0.15-$0.35/student. **Ease:** ★★★★★. **Engagement:** ★★★★★. **Internal:** P5/T5/V5/A5/S5/L5/E5/B5/R4/C4 = **96/100**.

**Lesson use:** Students observe gas production, temperature change, and new substances, then distinguish evidence of a chemical reaction from a simple mixture. A balloon-on-bottle variation makes the gas easier to see and measure.

**Best-deal plan:** Buy one large baking-soda box, one gallon of white vinegar, and a 30-50 pack of balloons only if using the balloon version. **Mini FAQ:** (1) *Is it safe?* Yes with normal eye protection and no tasting. (2) *Can we quantify it?* Yes - balloon circumference or mass change in an open system. (3) *Does it fit a chemical-change lesson?* Yes, as an evidence-and-explanation activity rather than a magic trick.

### 14. Oobleck / Non-Newtonian Fluid

**Status/TEKS:** Confirm before listing - match to the teacher’s current properties-of-matter unit; no public code yet. **Planning price:** $8-$18/class, $0.25-$0.60/student. **Ease:** ★★★★☆. **Engagement:** ★★★★★. **Internal:** P4/T2/V5/A5/S4/L4/E5/B4/R3/C3 = **72/100**. **Decision:** hold from public catalog until TEKS is confirmed; otherwise it is a strong low-cost engagement option.

**Mini FAQ:** (1) *Is cleanup hard?* It is manageable with trays and trash disposal, but should not enter drains. (2) *Is it a full lab?* Best as a 20-30 minute station plus data prompt. (3) *Why is it not listed yet?* The exact TEKS code needs teacher confirmation.

### 2. Leaf Chromatography

### 9. Punnett-Square Family Case

**Status/TEKS:** Confirm before listing - inheritance unit; do not publish a code until teacher confirms. **Planning price:** $0-$8/class. **Ease:** ★★★★★. **Engagement:** ★★★★☆. **Internal:** P5/T2/V3/A5/S5/L5/E5/B5/R5/C4 = **79/100**. **Decision:** use fictional traits/cases only and confirm TEKS before offering.

## Program-wide teacher FAQ

### Is BioBox free?

Yes. BioBox is a student-led service program. We aim to provide materials and facilitation at no cost when funding and inventory allow; a request is not a guarantee until we confirm the date and kit.

### What do you need from a teacher?

The course/unit, approximate student count, class length, available equipment, accessibility/safety constraints, and whether the teacher wants a demo, small-group investigation, or full lesson. We do not ask teachers to purchase materials for a confirmed BioBox activity.

### How much teacher preparation is required?

Very little for a standard session. We bring a short lesson flow, materials, setup/cleanup plan, and student handout where appropriate. The teacher remains the classroom authority and confirms that the activity fits district/school policies.

### How long does a visit take?

Most offerings can be shaped as a 10-15 minute demo, a 30-45 minute activity, or a full 45-55 minute investigation. Multi-day organism/growth activities are offered only when the teacher wants the follow-up.

### Is it safe?

We use age-appropriate, teacher-approved materials and state the safety needs before confirming a visit. We do not offer activities with uncontrolled cultures, unsealed mold, or real blood. Teachers can request a lower-risk alternative at any time.

### Can the activity be aligned to my exact lesson?

Yes. Tell us the grade/course and current TEKS/unit. We will suggest only activities with an appropriate fit; candidate activities stay off the public catalog until the teacher confirms the alignment.

### What if I only have 20 minutes, limited equipment, or a large class?

Tell us in the request. We can switch to a demonstration, a paper/data model, or pair-based stations. Many activities are designed for 30 students working in pairs, which keeps cost and setup manageable.

### What if I already have materials?

That helps. We can bring facilitation, student materials, or an extension rather than duplicate supplies. Please tell us what is available (for example, microscopes, balances, or a projector).

### Do you need photos or student names?

We never need student names. With the teacher’s and school’s permission, we may ask for a few non-identifying photos of materials, student hands/work, or the finished activity. Those photos and a short teacher quote help BioBox document impact for future grants, which helps keep the program free. We will respect any no-photo policy.

### Can I say no to photos and still receive a kit?

Yes. Photos are helpful, not a condition of participation. We can document impact with a teacher quote, anonymized student-work image, supply photo, or no media at all.

### Who supervises students?

The classroom teacher retains supervision and final control of the lesson. BioBox student volunteers support facilitation and setup; we follow the teacher’s directions and school rules.

### What happens after I request a kit?

We confirm the activity, timing, class size, supply availability, and safety needs. If the requested kit is not available, we suggest a close alternative or offer the Teacher Grant Help Program for supplies beyond our catalog.

### Can you help us find funding for a different lab or equipment request?

Yes. Through the Teacher Grant Help Program, we can help identify likely funding sources, provide direct links and deadlines, and help draft a short project description. We cannot promise funding or purchase, but we can make the search easier.

### Do you need a school purchase order or payment?

No payment is requested for a confirmed BioBox offering. If a teacher independently chooses to purchase supplies through a grant, that process remains between the teacher and the appropriate school/district channels.

## Internal procurement guardrails

- Do not order a kit until a teacher has confirmed class size, date, course/unit, and needed format.
- Prefer one shared class set or pair kit over 30 individual kits unless individual handling is necessary.
- Calculate **delivered cost per usable student activity**, including shipping, spare materials, and equipment that cannot be reused.
- Keep a 10-15% consumable buffer for spills, absences, and failed trials.
- Reuse durable items (trays, timers, pipettes, forceps, bottles, laminated cards) across activities.
- For an activity scoring below 80/100, require a specific teacher request, special educational value, or outside funding before purchasing.
- For every candidate TEKS activity, get written confirmation from the requesting teacher before displaying a standard on the website or in outreach.
