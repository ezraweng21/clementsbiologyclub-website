# BioBox pre-email audit

Last reviewed: August 25, 2026. This is an operational check, not a promise that every planning estimate is a current vendor quote.

## Email gate

Do not present a kit as available until all four are true:

1. Its TEKS is cross-checked against the club's supplied catalog and current course documents.
2. Its lesson uses the listed materials and produces the stated student evidence.
3. A specific supplier, quantity, shipping constraint, and per-class cost are recorded.
4. Safety, disposal, and the host teacher's required equipment are confirmed.

## Immediate decision: agar plates

**HOLD — do not offer in outreach yet.** The former site wording called environmental samples “safe.” Unknown environmental isolates are not inherently safe. ASM guidance allows environmental samples only under a teacher-led risk assessment; plates must stay sealed and be observed only, with no subculturing. The current Carolina 10-plate voucher is $43.05 before swabs, disposal supplies, or shipping, above the $35 per-class cap.

To restore this kit, choose one of these models in writing:

- a host teacher's established BSL-1 protocol, with sealed observation and school disposal; or
- a commercial, documented BSL-1 culture used under the host teacher's existing lab procedures.

Then record the exact vendor, pack size, number of plates/groups, cost, disposal method, and teacher approval.

## What to email now

Lead with the kits that are visually strong, closer to biology, inexpensive, and easiest for a teacher to say yes to once sourced: Foldscope, PTC (with opt-out), Brine Shrimp, Seed Germination, Balloon Lung, and DNA Extraction. State that the club will confirm materials, safety, and the final format with the teacher before scheduling.

Do not promise agar plates, mold, a specific price, live-organism delivery date, or a chemical concentration in the first email.
